AIR DEFAME 101

Game Design Update — UI, Radar Controls, Aircraft Identification & Enemy Radar Attacks

This update expands the existing HAWK air-defense simulation with:

- Main menu
- In-game radar controls
- Target cycling
- Target tracking
- Target locking
- Missile firing
- Radar range presets
- Improved target identification uncertainty
- Aircraft and missile radar icons
- Enemy air-to-ground missile attacks
- Anti-radiation missile attacks against the radar
- Radar damage and degraded radar range

The existing MIM-23A HAWK missile mechanics, ammunition system, launcher reload system, aircraft flight behavior, radar horizon, terrain masking, and two-target engagement limit should remain active.

---

1. GAME NAME

Change the game title to:

AIR DEFAME 101

Display this title prominently on the main menu.

3. IN-GAME BOTTOM CONTROL BAR

The main gameplay screen should have a permanent control bar along the bottom of the screen.

Buttons:

NEXT TARGET

TRACK

LOCK

FIRE

RANGE LIMIT

Example:

------------------------------------------------------------
 NEXT TARGET | TRACK | LOCK | FIRE | RANGE LIMIT: 40 KM
------------------------------------------------------------

These buttons must be large enough to work well on a touchscreen.

Each button should provide clear visual feedback when pressed.

---

4. NEXT TARGET

Button:

NEXT TARGET

Purpose:

Select the next closest detected radar contact.

Targets should normally be sorted by distance.

Example:

Current target:

TRACK 017
RANGE: 31.4 KM

Player presses:

NEXT TARGET

Radar selects:

TRACK 004
RANGE: 24.8 KM

Press again:

TRACK 022
RANGE: 18.1 KM

Continue cycling through detected contacts.

After reaching the final contact, return to the first target.

Only detected aircraft should be included.

Missiles may have separate icons but should not normally be selected using NEXT TARGET unless missile interception is added later.

---

5. TRACK

Button:

TRACK

TRACK tells the radar system to concentrate tracking calculations on the selected aircraft.

Tracking does NOT mean the aircraft has been selected for missile firing.

Tracking should:

- Add the aircraft to the priority target list.
- Increase measurement frequency.
- Improve speed estimation.
- Improve altitude estimation.
- Improve heading estimation.
- Improve size estimation.
- Improve identification confidence over time.
- Keep the aircraft highlighted.

TRACK should not produce a radar-lock warning for the enemy aircraft.

However, an aircraft with an RWR may still know that search radar energy exists in the area.

The important distinction is:

TRACK = surveillance / priority tracking

LOCK = fire-control radar lock

Example:

TRACK 014
STATUS: TRACKING
RANGE: 26.8 KM
ALT: 4,820 M
SPD: 890 KM/H
ID: UNKNOWN

After observing longer:

TRACK 014
STATUS: TRACKING
RANGE: 23.1 KM
ALT: 4,760 M
SPD: 905 KM/H
ID: POSSIBLE SU-25
CONFIDENCE: 47%

---

6. PRIORITY TARGET LIST

Tracked aircraft should be added to a priority list.

Example:

PRIORITY TARGETS

01  TRACK 014   POSSIBLE SU-25   23.1 KM
02  TRACK 021   UNKNOWN          28.7 KM
03  TRACK 009   LIKELY MIG-23    32.2 KM

Tracking multiple aircraft does NOT mean all of them are locked.

The HAWK system may continue observing several contacts while only engaging the limited number allowed by the fire-control system.

---

7. LOCK

Button:

LOCK

LOCK prepares the selected aircraft for missile engagement.

The target must normally be TRACKED before a lock can be established.

When LOCK is pressed:

1. Fire-control radar attempts to lock the aircraft.
2. The aircraft becomes an active engagement target.
3. The radar begins the illumination/fire-control process.
4. The target aircraft receives a radar-lock warning if its warning equipment supports it.
5. Enemy AI may begin defensive action.

Example:

TRACK 014

STATUS:
LOCKED

RANGE:
17.8 KM

TARGET:
PROBABLE SU-25

MISSILE:
READY

The selected target should receive a clear lock indicator.

For example:

[ LOCK ]

or:

LOCKED TARGET

The radar target icon could also become brighter or change shape.

---

8. TARGET LOCK WARNING

When an enemy aircraft becomes LOCKED, its AI should know that a fire-control radar is tracking it.

Depending on:

- Aircraft type
- Pilot skill
- Range
- Mission behavior
- Current attack objective

the aircraft may react.

Possible reactions:

Turn away

Dive

Accelerate

Begin terrain masking

Perform defensive maneuvers

Continue attacking despite the warning

Prepare countermeasures

The aircraft should not always react identically.

---

9. FIRE

Button:

FIRE

FIRE launches a MIM-23A HAWK missile at the currently locked target.

Requirements:

Target must be locked.

A launcher must have a missile available.

The target must be within the permitted missile engagement envelope.

One of the two engagement channels must be available.

Example:

TARGET LOCKED
RANGE 16.4 KM

M192-1
3 MISSILES READY

[FIRE]

After pressing FIRE:

M192-1
2 MISSILES READY

MISSILE AWAY

Show the launched missile on radar.

11. SHORTER RANGE = HIGHER PRECISION

For gameplay, shorter radar range settings should provide more accurate information.

This represents the radar system concentrating its display and processing on a smaller area.

Suggested values:

10 KM

Highest precision.

Very accurate:

Range

Speed

Altitude

Heading

Target size

Aircraft identification

---

20 KM

High precision.

Small measurement errors.

---

30 KM

Medium precision.

Aircraft identification becomes less reliable.

---

40 KM

Lowest precision.

More uncertainty in:

Speed

Altitude

Size

Target identification

This setting is excellent for detecting approaching threats but not as good for identifying them.

---

12. RADAR RANGE ACCURACY MULTIPLIER

Suggested starting values:

10 KM = 100% measurement quality
20 KM = 92%
30 KM = 82%
40 KM = 70%

These values can be adjusted after gameplay testing.

Distance to the actual target should still matter.

A target at 38 km should be harder to identify than a target at 8 km.

If the radar does not have enough information to identify the aircraft, use a generic aircraft/contact symbol.

Example:

      ◇
      ?
    T-014

or:

UNKNOWN

Do NOT immediately show the actual aircraft icon.

The correct aircraft silhouette should become available only when identification confidence becomes sufficiently high.

---

15. IDENTIFIED AIRCRAFT ICONS

Create different top-down icons for:

Su-25

MiG-21

MiG-23

Su-22

Su-17

The icons should be simplified radar-style silhouettes rather than detailed aircraft images.

The player should eventually be able to recognize different aircraft by icon shape.

However, the icon should only change from UNKNOWN to a specific aircraft after the radar identification system becomes sufficiently confident.

---

16. UNKNOWN FACTOR

Add an explicit UNKNOWN FACTOR to target identification.

Radar identification should NEVER be guaranteed merely because the game internally knows what aircraft was spawned.

Each target maintains several identification probabilities.

Example:

TRACK 014

SU-25       52%
SU-17       18%
SU-22       16%
MIG-21       5%
MIG-23       2%
UNKNOWN       7%

The UNKNOWN percentage represents information that the radar cannot confidently explain.

At long range:

MIG-23      32%
SU-17       20%
SU-22       18%
UNKNOWN     30%

At close range:

MIG-23      94%
UNKNOWN      3%
OTHER        3%

---

17. WHAT INCREASES THE UNKNOWN FACTOR?

Increase UNKNOWN when:

Target is far away.

Target is flying extremely low.

Target is partially hidden by terrain.

Ground clutter is strong.

The target is maneuvering heavily.

The radar has only observed it briefly.

Target size measurement is unreliable.

Multiple aircraft have similar characteristics.

The player is using a long radar range setting.

---

18. WHAT DECREASES THE UNKNOWN FACTOR?

Decrease UNKNOWN when:

Target gets closer.

Target remains tracked longer.

Target climbs higher.

Radar line-of-sight improves.

Player changes to a shorter range setting.

Speed becomes stable.

Altitude becomes stable.

Aircraft dimensions become easier to estimate.

More movement history becomes available.

21. ENEMY AIR-TO-GROUND ATTACKS

Some enemy aircraft should be capable of attacking the HAWK battery.

Not every aircraft can do this equally well.

Aircraft capabilities should follow the War Thunder versions being used for the game.

---

22. MIG-21BIS

The MiG-21bis should NOT carry guided air-to-ground missiles in this mission.

It may:

Escort strike aircraft.

Attack HAWK aircraft interceptors if those are added later.

Distract the radar operator.

Force the player to decide which target is most dangerous.

The MiG-21 can therefore appear dangerous because of its speed even though another slower strike aircraft may represent the larger threat to the radar site.

---

27. ANTI-RADIATION MISSILE LOGIC

Anti-radiation missiles are different from ordinary air-to-ground missiles.

They search for radar emissions.

The gameplay concept should be:

HAWK RADAR TRANSMITS
        ↓
ENEMY SEAD AIRCRAFT DETECTS RADAR
        ↓
RADAR LOCATION ESTIMATED
        ↓
ANTI-RADIATION MISSILE LAUNCHED
        ↓
MISSILE HOMES TOWARD RADAR

This means operating the radar should have a consequence.

For the first version, do not make this system excessively complicated.

The enemy AI can simply calculate whether:

- The radar is active.
- Aircraft has an anti-radiation weapon.
- Aircraft is within its launch conditions.
- Radar signal is detectable.

Then decide whether to fire.

30. AIRCRAFT ATTACK BEHAVIOR

Enemy strike aircraft should not launch immediately after spawning.

They should:

1. Enter radar coverage.
2. Navigate toward the target area.
3. Search for the HAWK site.
4. Reach acceptable missile-launch conditions.
5. Launch.
6. Turn away or begin defensive maneuvering.

Different aircraft should behave differently.

---

31. SU-25 ATTACK BEHAVIOR

The Su-25 should normally:

Fly relatively low.

Use terrain.

Approach closer.

Launch Kh-25ML or Kh-29L.

Turn away after launch.

It may need to continue laser designation depending on the missile.

This makes the Su-25 vulnerable to the HAWK during the attack.

---

32. MIG-23M ATTACK BEHAVIOR

A MiG-23 carrying a Kh-23M should:

Approach quickly.

Launch from relatively short range.

Maintain attention on the target while guiding.

Have reduced ability to perform aggressive defensive maneuvers during missile guidance.

After the missile hits or misses, it may immediately accelerate away.

---

33. SU-17 / SU-22 ATTACK BEHAVIOR

Su-17 and Su-22 aircraft should be more likely to perform SEAD missions.

Typical behavior:

Fly low.

Approach using terrain.

Detect radar emission.

Climb briefly if required.

Launch anti-radiation missile.

Turn away.

Descend.

Exit the HAWK engagement zone.

This creates an important priority decision for the player.

A MiG-21 may be closer.

But the Su-17 farther away may actually be the aircraft preparing to destroy the radar.

---

34. RADAR BASE DAMAGE

Enemy missiles can damage the HAWK battery.

For this version, use a simplified damage system.

Every successful missile hit reduces the radar's maximum effective detection range.

Use a randomized damage value.

Suggested starting value:

2–8 km radar range lost per missile hit.

Example:

Starting radar:

MAX RADAR RANGE
40 KM

Missile hits:

RADAR HIT

DAMAGE:
-6 KM

New maximum:

MAX RADAR RANGE
34 KM

Second missile:

RADAR HIT

DAMAGE:
-4 KM

New range:

30 KM

The exact damage amount should be randomized for now.

39. PRIORITY THREAT LOGIC

The radar should NOT automatically tell the player exactly which enemy must be destroyed first.

However, the system can calculate a general threat level.

Possible factors:

Aircraft range.

Aircraft heading.

Aircraft speed.

Aircraft identification.

Whether aircraft is approaching the base.

Whether aircraft is carrying air-to-ground missiles.

Whether aircraft has launched a weapon.

Example:

TRACK 017
MIG-21
THREAT: MEDIUM

TRACK 022
SU-17
THREAT: HIGH

TRACK 031
SU-25
THREAT: HIGH

Do not reveal an aircraft's weapons before the radar has enough information.

46. CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The player should constantly make decisions with incomplete information.

Example:

The radar shows three contacts:

TRACK 014
POSSIBLE MIG-21
15 KM

TRACK 021
UNKNOWN
24 KM

TRACK 033
POSSIBLE SU-17
31 KM

The MiG-21 is closest.

However:

TRACK 033 may be an Su-17 carrying an anti-radiation missile.

The player must decide:

Do I engage the closest aircraft?

Do I wait for better identification?

Do I track the distant aircraft?

Do I use one of my two engagement channels?

Do I risk allowing the strike aircraft to launch?

This uncertainty should be one of the main features of Air Defame 101.
