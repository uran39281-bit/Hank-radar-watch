HAWK Air Defense Radar Game — Mission 1

For this version of the game, the player will operate a HAWK air-defense battery equipped with MIM-23A HAWK missiles.

The goal is to make the radar, target identification, missile guidance, aircraft behavior, and engagement system feel believable while still being fun to play.

---

1. MIM-23A HAWK SYSTEM

Use the MIM-23A HAWK as the primary surface-to-air missile.

MIM-23A Reference Performance

Characteristic| Value
Maximum engagement range| 25 km
Flight ceiling| ~13,700 m
Maximum speed| ~Mach 2.5
Launch weight| ~584 kg / 1,290 lb
Warhead| ~54 kg / 119 lb
Warhead type| HE blast-fragmentation
Guidance| Semi-active radar homing
Launcher| M192 triple missile launcher

The MIM-23A uses semi-active radar guidance, meaning the HAWK battery must continue illuminating the target for the missile to remain properly guided.

For gameplay, the fire-control system may engage a maximum of two targets simultaneously.

The player may track more aircraft on the radar, but only two aircraft may be actively illuminated/engaged at the same time.

---

2. MISSILE INVENTORY

At the beginning of Mission 1, the battery has:

9 missiles ready to fire

and

9 additional missiles in reserve.

Use three M192 triple launchers.

Each launcher begins with:

3 × MIM-23A missiles

Therefore:

3 launchers × 3 missiles = 9 ready missiles

Reserve ammunition:

9 missiles

Total battery ammunition:

18 missiles

---

3. RELOADING SYSTEM

An M192 launcher contains three missiles.

A launcher cannot be reloaded until all three missiles on that launcher have been fired.

Example:

Launcher #1:

MISSILE 1 — FIRED
MISSILE 2 — READY
MISSILE 3 — READY

The launcher CANNOT reload.

After:

MISSILE 1 — FIRED
MISSILE 2 — FIRED
MISSILE 3 — FIRED

the launcher becomes:

EMPTY — RELOADING

Reload time:

90 seconds

During those 90 seconds, that launcher cannot fire.

After the reload completes, three missiles are removed from the reserve ammunition supply and placed on the launcher.

If fewer than three reserve missiles remain, follow whatever ammunition rule is appropriate for the mission design.

- Su-25

---

5. AIRCRAFT MANEUVERABILITY

Do NOT make every aircraft fly the same way.

Each aircraft should have different acceleration, turning, altitude, and energy characteristics.

Su-25

Role: Ground attack aircraft

The Su-25 should normally be one of the slowest aircraft.

It should commonly operate at low or medium altitude.

It has a relatively large physical profile because of its broad wings.

Its combat maneuverability should be lower than dedicated fighters.

It can perform defensive turns, dives, and terrain-hugging maneuvers, but it should not suddenly behave like a MiG-21.

---

MiG-21bis

Role: Fighter

The MiG-21 should be:

Fast
Small
Highly responsive
Capable of aggressive defensive maneuvers

Its narrow 7.2 m wingspan makes its physical size profile noticeably different from the Su-25 or Su-17/Su-22.

The MiG-21 should be capable of aggressive instantaneous turns but should lose energy during sustained hard maneuvering.

---

MiG-23M

Role: Fighter / interceptor

The MiG-23 should normally be extremely fast.

Its variable-geometry wings mean that its apparent size and maneuvering characteristics can change depending on wing sweep.

At high speed the wings should normally be swept backward.

At lower speeds the aircraft can increase wing area for maneuvering.

The MiG-23 should generally behave like a high-speed interceptor rather than a slow attack aircraft.

---

Su-17M4 / Su-22M3

Role: Strike aircraft

These aircraft should be deliberately difficult for the radar identification system to distinguish.

Both aircraft have very similar dimensions:

Length: approximately 18.9 m
Wingspan: approximately 10–13.7 m

Their performance is also similar.

Therefore, when their speed, altitude, and range are similar, the radar identification computer should frequently confuse:

Su-17 ↔ Su-22

This is intentional.

---

6. RADAR TARGET DETECTION

The radar must NOT automatically know what every aircraft is.

A newly detected contact should initially appear as something such as:

UNKNOWN

UNKNOWN AIR CONTACT

TRACK 014

TRACK 021

etc.

The radar measures or estimates:

Range
Altitude
Speed
Estimated aircraft size
Heading
Vertical speed
Movement behavior

These measurements are then used to estimate what aircraft the contact might be.

---

7. TARGET IDENTIFICATION SYSTEM

Target identification should depend primarily on:

ALTITUDE + SPEED + SIZE + RANGE

Additional behavior information can improve identification after the aircraft has been observed for longer.

The computer compares the radar measurements against known aircraft performance profiles.

Example:

A radar contact is:

Altitude: 4,800 m
Speed: 870 km/h
Estimated size: Medium/Large
Range: 11 km

The computer might determine:

SU-25 — 72%
SU-17 — 14%
SU-22 — 10%
UNKNOWN — 4%

Instead of immediately displaying:

SU-25

display:

PROBABLE SU-25 — 72%

---

8. RADAR IDENTIFICATION ERRORS

The identification system MUST sometimes be wrong.

Aircraft with similar characteristics should create uncertainty.

For example:

A Su-17 and MiG-21 might temporarily produce similar speed and altitude measurements.

At long range, inaccurate size measurements could make the radar computer incorrectly classify the Su-17 as a MiG-21.

As the aircraft approaches, the estimated dimensions become more accurate and the identification may change:

UNKNOWN

↓

POSSIBLE MIG-21

↓

POSSIBLE SU-17

↓

PROBABLE SU-17

↓

SU-17 — HIGH CONFIDENCE

Do not make identification completely deterministic.

Add small measurement errors to:

Speed
Altitude
Range
Estimated target size

The amount of measurement error should increase with distance.

---

9. RANGE VS IDENTIFICATION ACCURACY

The closer an aircraft is to the radar, the easier it should be to identify.

The farther away it is, the less reliable the identification becomes.

For example:

Very Close Range

Radar measurements are highly accurate.

Size estimation becomes much better.

Identification confidence rises quickly.

Medium Range

Speed and altitude remain useful.

Size estimation becomes less accurate.

Two similar aircraft may be confused.

Long Range

Identification should become much less reliable.

The radar may only display something like:

FAST AIRCRAFT

FIGHTER-SIZED CONTACT

LARGE STRIKE AIRCRAFT

UNKNOWN

The missile engagement range and the radar detection range should NOT automatically be the same value.

The radar may detect aircraft beyond the missile's effective engagement range.

---

10. SIZE-BASED IDENTIFICATION

Physical aircraft dimensions should act as a gameplay approximation for target size.

Do NOT treat physical dimensions as an exact radar cross section.

For identification purposes:

Small Profile

MiG-21

Length: 14.1 m
Wingspan: 7.2 m

Medium Profile

MiG-23

Length: 16.7 m
Variable wingspan: 7.8–14 m

Large Profile

Su-25

Length: 14.2 m
Wingspan: 14.4 m

Large Profile

Su-17 / Su-22

Length: 18.9 m
Variable wingspan: 10–13.7 m

Aircraft orientation should also influence the estimated size.

A target flying directly toward the radar should not appear identical to the same aircraft flying broadside.

---

11. LOW-ALTITUDE RADAR DETECTION

Aircraft flying near the ground should be significantly harder to detect and track.

Model three major effects:

Earth curvature
Terrain obstruction
Ground clutter

The radar should require line-of-sight to the aircraft.

Mountains, hills, or terrain between the radar and target should block or degrade detection.

An aircraft flying extremely low may disappear behind terrain and reappear later.

---

12. RADAR HORIZON

Use a simplified radar-horizon calculation.

A useful approximation is:

Radar Horizon ≈ 4.12 × (√Radar Height + √Target Height)

where:

Heights are measured in meters.

Result is approximately kilometers.

This prevents extremely low aircraft from being detected unrealistically far away.

Example concept:

A fighter at 8,000 m altitude can be detected much farther away than the same fighter flying at 50 m above the ground.

---

13. GROUND CLUTTER

Even when an aircraft is theoretically above the radar horizon, very low targets should still be more difficult to track.

Radar quality should decrease when an aircraft flies close to terrain.

Suggested gameplay behavior:

Above 1,500 m AGL:

Little or no ground-clutter penalty.

500–1,500 m AGL:

Small tracking penalty.

150–500 m AGL:

Moderate tracking and identification penalty.

Below 150 m AGL:

Major detection and identification penalty.

Aircraft flying through valleys should sometimes temporarily disappear from radar.

When this happens, retain the track's last known position briefly rather than instantly deleting it.

Display something such as:

TRACK LOST

or

COASTING

The radar may attempt to reacquire the target.

---

14. RADAR IDENTIFICATION CONFIDENCE

Create an internal confidence system.

A possible starting formula is:

Identification Confidence =

30% Speed Match

+ 25% Size Match
+ 20% Altitude/Profile Match
+ 15% Aircraft Behavior Match
+ 10% Track History

Then multiply the result by:

Range Quality

and

Radar Signal Quality

Add a small randomized measurement error.

The exact numbers can be adjusted during gameplay testing.

The important requirement is:

Distance and low altitude must make identification less reliable.

---

15. CONFIDENCE DISPLAY

Use several identification states.

0–30%:

UNKNOWN

30–50%:

POSSIBLE [AIRCRAFT]

50–75%:

LIKELY [AIRCRAFT]

75–90%:

PROBABLE [AIRCRAFT]

90%+:

HIGH CONFIDENCE [AIRCRAFT]

Confidence should be capable of going DOWN as well as up.

Example:

PROBABLE MIG-21 — 82%

The aircraft slows down and begins behaving unlike a MiG-21.

The computer changes to:

POSSIBLE SU-17 — 58%

After additional radar observations:

PROBABLE SU-17 — 91%

This will make the radar system feel like it is actually analyzing the aircraft.

---

16. AIRCRAFT EVASIVE MANEUVERS

Enemy aircraft must be able to react to missile engagements.

Aircraft should NOT simply fly straight toward the target after a missile has been launched.

Depending on aircraft type, altitude, speed, AI skill, and situation, the aircraft may:

Break left or right
Perform a hard turn
Dive toward terrain
Climb aggressively
Change heading
Accelerate
Reduce altitude
Use terrain masking
Perform repeated defensive maneuvers

The maneuver must remain inside the aircraft's performance capabilities.

Do NOT allow instantaneous direction changes.

Do NOT allow unrealistic unlimited G.

Do NOT allow aircraft to maintain full speed while performing extreme turns.

Energy should matter.

---

17. AIRCRAFT-SPECIFIC DEFENSIVE AI

MiG-21

Aggressive turns.

High instantaneous maneuverability.

Rapid energy loss during sustained hard turning.

Likely to make sharp defensive direction changes.

MiG-23

Uses speed aggressively.

May accelerate away from the engagement.

Can perform high-speed defensive turns.

Wing geometry should change depending on flight condition if the simulation supports it.

Su-25

Less likely to escape through raw speed.

More likely to dive low, turn toward terrain, or attempt terrain masking.

Su-17 / Su-22

Prefer high-speed evasive maneuvers.

May dive toward low altitude and attempt to leave the HAWK engagement zone.

---

18. MISSILE GUIDANCE

The MIM-23A uses semi-active radar homing.

The missile should therefore depend on radar illumination from the HAWK fire-control system.

Basic engagement sequence:

Radar detects target

↓

Target track established

↓

Player selects target

↓

Target illumination begins

↓

MISSILE READY

↓

MISSILE LAUNCH

↓

Missile accelerates toward target

↓

Target may maneuver

↓

Radar continues target illumination

↓

Either:

INTERCEPT

or

MISS

If illumination is lost for too long, missile guidance quality should degrade significantly.

---

19. MISSILE INTERCEPT LOGIC

Do not determine missile hits using a simple random percentage.

Missile success should depend on:

Target range
Target speed
Target heading
Target altitude
Target maneuvering
Missile remaining energy
Line of sight
Radar illumination
Terrain
Launch geometry

A missile launched against a slow aircraft flying directly toward the battery should generally have a better intercept opportunity than a missile launched against a fast aircraft crossing near the edge of the engagement envelope.

---

20. MISSILE FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

The missile should NOT instantly travel at Mach 2.5.

Model an acceleration phase.

The missile accelerates after launch, reaches high speed, and eventually begins losing energy.

Missile maneuverability should therefore change during flight.

At long range, the missile should have less energy available for large last-second turns.

This makes aircraft evasive maneuvers and launch range meaningful.

---

21. TWO-TARGET ENGAGEMENT LIMIT

The HAWK battery may actively engage no more than:

2 aircraft simultaneously.

Example:

TRACK 014 — ENGAGED
TRACK 021 — ENGAGED
TRACK 027 — TRACKING
TRACK 031 — TRACKING
TRACK 044 — TRACKING

The player can still see TRACK 027, TRACK 031, and TRACK 044.

However, another missile engagement cannot begin until one of the two active engagement channels becomes available.

---

22. IMPORTANT GAMEPLAY PRINCIPLE

The player should never feel like the radar has perfect knowledge.

The gameplay loop should be:

Detect

→ Track

→ Estimate

→ Identify

→ Prioritize

→ Illuminate

→ Launch

→ Maintain track

→ Observe target reaction

→ Confirm kill or miss

This should create uncertainty.

A target may appear to be a MiG-21 but later turn out to be a Su-17.

A low-altitude Su-25 may disappear behind terrain.

A distant MiG-23 may initially appear only as:

FAST UNKNOWN CONTACT

A Su-17 and Su-22 should sometimes remain difficult to distinguish for most of their approach.

The radar operator should have to make decisions using incomplete information instead of simply clicking aircraft names.

---

23. MISSION 1 DESIGN GOAL

Mission 1 should introduce the player to:

Radar scanning
Target tracking
Identification uncertainty
Low-altitude detection
Radar horizon
Terrain masking
Target prioritization
Two-target engagement limits
MIM-23A missile launches
Semi-active radar guidance
Missile ammunition management
90-second launcher reloads
Aircraft evasive behavior

Start the mission slowly with only a few contacts.

As the mission progresses, introduce aircraft at different:

Altitudes
Speeds
Ranges
Headings

Eventually create situations where several aircraft appear simultaneously and the player must decide which two targets are dangerous enough to engage first.

The radar and identification systems should provide information, but the final engagement decision belongs to the player.
